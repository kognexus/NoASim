# NX-28: Coordinated damping between neighbours

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
From a colleague's Lyapunov derivation of nearest-neighbour damping, reviewed with the
owner: its relative-angle coupling is a spring, not a damper, but the relative-velocity
form, a damper between neighbouring joints, is passive by construction with fresh data.
NX-27 found about 3× headroom over network damping, all of it from coordinating the
joints. This ticket asks how much of that a neighbour coupling can reach.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Modes no joint can reach: for each flexible mode of each arm, how strongly each
  joint's torque drives it and each joint's sensors see it, from the linearised plant;
  any mode no joint reaches is named
- [x] The ceiling of a neighbour coupling: the best symmetric relative-velocity gains
  between neighbours (on top of network damping), against network damping and the ideal
  controller, on the NX-27 taps
- [x] If the ceiling is worth it (a clear share of the NX-27 headroom), the coupling as a
  damping mode in the node, inside the energy tank, measured on taps, tasks and with bus
  delay; if not, the finding and why
- [x] A neighbour's weight in the network term fades with the age of its data instead of
  switching off at 2 ms; no torque steps under intermittent frame loss (steps under
  loss with jitter cut from 4.4 to 0.9 N·m; under loss alone unchanged, see the log)
- [x] ADR-0004 restated in energy and graph-Laplacian terms: what the clean argument
  covers (fresh data) and what the energy tank adds (delay, loss)

## Tests

- `sim/src/util/matrix.test.ts`: Cholesky and symmetric eigenvalues
- `sim/src/analysis/reach.test.ts`: on the light arm, some joint adds at least 5 %
  damping to every mode below 10 Hz, and no joint reaches its link bending above 50 Hz
- `sim/src/harness/network.test.ts`: on a bus losing 20 % of frames with 4 cycles of
  jitter, the network torque never steps by 1.5 N·m in a cycle (4.4 before), and a tap
  still settles within 0.35 s
- Rust: `freshness` falls from 1 to 0 between 5 and 8 cycles, monotonically

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **Modes and reach** (`sim/src/analysis/reach.ts`, "Reach" in RESULTS.md).
  The held arm is not conservative (the position loop pushes on the motor and measures
  the output: 35–46 % of its stiffness is skew), so a symmetric modal analysis gave wrong
  modes; the analysis uses the full state matrix from MuJoCo's continuous accelerations
  (one integrator step mixed the implicit gear damping into the stiffness) and finds its
  eigenvalues by inverse iteration over a grid of shifts. For each mode and joint, the
  first-order damping that joint adds alone, with its own rate and with its network
  signal. Findings:
  - Below 15 Hz every mode is a joint mode, 24–56 % damped by the position loops, and
    reached by some joint (up to 48 %). The network signal reaches them about as well as
    the joint's own rate: one reason network and joint-local damping settle a tap about
    equally (H1).
  - On the light arm, link bending at 55 Hz (home) and 66–69 Hz (reach), 85–98 % bending,
    is reached by no joint (at most 0.4 %, J1, with the wrong sign): it bends across every
    joint axis. Only the structure's own damping (13–16 %) acts on it. The stiff arm has
    no such mode below 80 Hz.
  - Before its 15 Hz low-pass, J2's network signal would feed several 35–70 Hz modes
    (−7 to −19 %); the low-pass cuts that signal to 4–16 % with 130–155° of lag, and the
    tank bounds what is left. This is the mechanism behind the 120 Hz ring in ADR-0004.
- **The ceiling of a neighbour coupling** (taps as in NX-27; "Headroom" in RESULTS.md):

| Variant | KX-6L mean / within 0.5 mm | KX-6 mean / within 0.5 mm |
|---|---|---|
| Network damping | 1.135 mm / 0.216 s | 0.990 mm / 0.225 s |
| + neighbour damper, κ = 0.1 | 1.102 mm / 0.216 s | 0.969 mm / 0.232 s |
| + neighbour damper, κ = 0.3 | 1.073 mm / 0.246 s | 0.938 mm / 0.248 s |
| + neighbour damper, κ = 1 | 2.84 mm / 1.61 s | 1.55 mm / not within 3 s |
| Ideal, neighbours only | 0.555 mm / 0.821 s | 0.757 mm / 1.303 s |
| Ideal, two joints either side | 0.423 mm / 0.097 s | 0.379 mm / 0.442 s |
| Ideal, whole arm | 0.385 mm / 0.077 s | 0.266 mm / 0.079 s |

  The neighbour damper (the colleague's law in its relative-velocity form, on true
  velocities with no delay) reaches 7–8 % of the headroom at best, and strong coupling
  makes the arm ring for over a second although it is passive. Not built into the node.
  What matters is information: the ideal gain cut to each joint's neighbours keeps part
  of the advantage with a slow tail; two joints either side gets most of it. (Cutting an
  LQR gain is a crude design; a gain designed for that structure would do at least as
  well, so these are lower bounds for what each reach allows.)
- **Fading stale neighbours** (`damping.rs`). A neighbour's weight is 1 up to 5 cycles
  (normal latency and jitter), falls linearly to 0 at the existing 8-cycle limit, and is
  smoothed (20 Hz) so it follows sustained staleness rather than each frame. The torque
  blends network and local damping by it; the last network signal fades out instead of
  dropping. Tap on the light arm, three seeds, largest one-cycle step of the network
  torque on J1–J4, settling time:

| Bus | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Clean | 0.24 N·m, 0.245 s | 0.24 N·m, 0.245 s |
| 20 % lost | 1.33 N·m, 0.266 s | 1.33 N·m, 0.267 s |
| Jitter 4 cycles | 0.24 N·m, 0.239 s | 0.24 N·m, 0.244 s |
| 20 % lost, jitter 4 | 4.42 N·m, 0.265 s | 0.91 N·m, 0.273 s |

  Tried and dropped: fading from 2 cycles on (the weight swung with every frame under
  jitter: steps of 10 N·m, settling 0.34 s), and pairing the neighbour's gyro with this
  joint's own from the same cycle (no measurable difference). The 1.3 N·m steps under
  loss alone do not come from switching; not traced further.
- **Proof re-run** (35 min): every verdict as before; the fade moves the KX-6's network
  tap by 0.002 mm (H1 against local 1.00 → 1.01×).
- **ADR-0004** gains a section with the argument in energy terms: collocated damping
  and a neighbour damper are dissipative by structure (a weighted graph Laplacian), the
  network term is not, which is what the tank is for; what the bus breaks; and what
  LaSalle needs, which the reach analysis now measures.
