# NX-26: Budget and premium actuator grades

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
Asked for by the owner: how does the system behave on a budget actuator with strong
cogging (a low-cost QDD module) against a high-quality one?

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Actuator grades in the sim, each a set of drawn imperfections around the
  datasheet: premium (low cogging and ripple, tight current sensing) and budget (strong
  cogging, phase-current offset and gain mismatch, more friction and gear play)
- [x] Phase-current sensing errors are modelled where they act: per-phase offset gives
  torque ripple at the electrical frequency, gain mismatch at twice it, and the joint
  measures the current it thinks it drives, not the true one
- [x] The phase current the joint measures is part of its state, available to its own
  models (PHNN residual, report to source)
- [x] A per-grade proof: tap settling, task tracking and vibration, report to source,
  with and without each joint's learned model; reported per grade in RESULTS.md
- [x] Demo: actuator grade selectable

## Tests

- Unit tests: offset gives the 1× electrical ripple, gain mismatch the 2×, at the
  expected amplitude
- Grades drawn deterministically per seed

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **Grades** (`sim/src/actuators/grades.ts`). A grade is drawn like a hidden
  family, but every joint gets every imperfection at the grade's level: friction
  (budget with a Stribeck hump), cogging and ripple at the sixth electrical order,
  current-sensor offset and gain error, gear play (budget only) and torque-constant
  drift. Levels are our estimates for the two ends of the market, relative to the
  datasheet, not measurements of a product. Grades are drawn only when named, so every
  earlier draw (and every proof number) is unchanged.
- **Current sensing** (`effects.ts`). The drive regulates the current it measures, so a
  sensor error becomes a torque error the joint does not see in its current reading: an
  offset gives a load-independent ripple once per electrical turn (amplitude = offset ×
  Kt), a gain mismatch a load-proportional ripple twice per turn plus a mean
  torque-constant error. Unit tests check both harmonics and amplitudes. The measured
  current was already part of each joint's state and of its motor balance (the PHNN's
  input and the report-to-source residual), so nothing changed there.
- **Learned models see the electrical order.** The PHNN's cogging features started at
  the gear mesh; neither current-sensing ripple was representable. A unit knows its pole
  pairs, so the features now include once per electrical turn (and, as doubles, twice).
  Each weight file carries its own orders, and the loader reads them, so the committed
  open and sealed models are unchanged. Held-out error (N·m RMS, mean of six joints):

| Unit | Datasheet | RLS fit | Learned, mesh orders | Learned, + electrical | Truth |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Premium (kx6-premium-s600) | 0.474 | 0.417 | 0.405 (37 % of the gap) | 0.290 (101 %) | 0.291 |
| Budget (kx6-budget-s500) | 44.83 | 28.33 | 0.978 (99 %) | 0.552 (100 %) | 0.372 |

  Premium joint 2 ended worse than its datasheet without the electrical order (−18 %);
  with it, 96 % of its gap closes.
- **Per grade in closed loop** (`experiments/src/grades.ts`, 5 min; in RESULTS.md as
  "Grades"). KX-6, VLA computer, each joint's datasheet model unless noted:

| | Ideal | Premium | Budget | Budget, learned models |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tap settling, joint control only / network | 0.515 / 0.270 s | 0.506 / 0.261 s | 0.453 / 0.696 s | 0.394 / 0.228 s |
| Figure of eight, joint control only / network | 2.60 / 2.59 mm | 2.42 / 2.39 mm | 25.1 / 25.3 mm | 5.37 / 5.42 mm |
| Task vibration J2–J6, joint control only / network | 7.55 / 7.17 °/s | 8.10 / 7.46 °/s | 21.3 / 23.3 °/s | 20.2 / 21.6 °/s |
| Sweep deviation from the ideal arm, datasheet / learned | — | 0.129 / 0.065 mrad | 29.5 / 4.76 mrad | |
| Attribution, datasheet / learned residual (of 10) | 10 / — | 10 / 10 | 3 / 6 | |
| Claims with nothing injected, datasheet / learned | none | none / none | J1 J2 J3 J5 J6 / J6 | |

- **Premium behaves like the ideal arm.** Network damping halves tap settling and lowers
  task vibration; attribution is perfect; the learned model halves the small sweep
  deviation.
- **Budget: a bug, found by the owner.** "Even with network damping the budget arm has
  the same error as joint control only; something isn't right." Right. The 25 mm was
  not vibration: 25.1 mm of it was below 3 Hz, 1.7 mm above, and the joints' output
  motion above 20 Hz was the same as on the ideal arm (an earlier reading of this log
  blamed rattle in the play; that was wrong, the rattle stays on the motor side). The
  arm stood 18 mrad (J2) and 25 mrad (J3) low before any task. Cause: each joint
  estimated the torque it holds as datasheet gear stiffness × gear twist, and with play
  the twist includes half the play, which a stiff gear turns into tens of N·m that are
  not there. The payload estimate built on it read 12.7 kg instead of 3.0 kg, and the
  gravity feed-forward held the arm low. Removing only the play brought the estimate to
  3.2 kg and the error to 2.1 mm. Damping cannot touch a slow offset; that is why
  network and joint control only were the same.
- **Fix.** The joint now takes the torque its gear transmits from its own model of
  itself (`DynamicsModel::spring_torque`): the datasheet spring for the datasheet and
  fitted models, the learned gear (play and stiffening included) for the learned one.
  Nothing changes with datasheet models or datasheet actuators. On the budget arm with
  its learned models the payload reads 4.8 kg, the sag is 3–4 mrad, the figure of eight
  5.4 mm instead of 25, and network damping again settles a tap in 58 % of the time of
  joint control only, as on the other arms. A joint with the datasheet model still reads
  play as load; it has no way to know better, which is the case for learning the model.
- **Protocol fix.** The learned-model trials switched to the learned model after 3 s of
  settling on the datasheet one; the payload estimate, averaged only while the arm
  stands still, kept what it had learned with the wrong model. A joint with a learned
  model uses it from power-on, so the trials now do too (`learned-models.ts`,
  `grades.ts`); H10b is re-measured in the final proof run.
- **What remains on budget actuators.** The 5.4 mm left is again slow (5.2 mm below
  3 Hz): the payload still reads 60 % high and the joints track through stiction and
  play. Network damping trims only the fast part (1.42 → 1.23 mm), and adds 7 % task
  vibration. Attribution with the learned residual is 6 of 10.
