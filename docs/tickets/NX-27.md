# NX-27: Headroom for learned damping

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
Asked for by the owner: could reinforcement learning damp better than the fixed law?
Before training a policy, measure how much better any controller could do.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] An ideal reference controller that sees the plant's full state (every joint, every
  link's bending) with no bus delay, as an upper bound no real joint can reach
- [x] The same per-joint torque limits and passivity budget as the joints have
- [x] Measured against joint control only, joint-local and network damping on tap
  settling (both arms, datasheet and budget actuators); task vibration not bounded, see
  the log
- [x] A short finding: how much headroom is left for a learned damper, and where
  (which disturbances, which grades); a recommendation whether RL is worth it

## Tests

- `sim/src/util/matrix.test.ts`: inverse, scalar Riccati equation, LQR on a double
  integrator
- `sim/src/analysis/ideal.test.ts`: the ideal controller holds the KX-6L under 30 % of
  the undamped mean distance after a tap, inside its torque budget, deterministically

- The reference controller is deterministic per seed and its runs are part of the
  experiments, not the node

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **The ideal controller** (`sim/src/analysis/ideal.ts`). The plant is
  linearised where the arm holds (central finite differences on the MuJoCo state, one
  bus cycle per step; `Plant.state`/`setState`), the joints' own position loops are
  folded in, and a discrete LQR (`sim/src/util/matrix.ts`, doubling algorithm) weights
  the tool's distance and speed against torque. It acts through
  `ArmSystem.outsideTorque`, a hook for experiments only, with the joints' damping
  budget: a quarter of peak torque per joint and the same 50 mJ energy tank on the true
  output velocity. Its weighting is picked per arm on one tuning tap (12 tried), as the
  joints' gains were.
- **Scoring.** H1's settling time is relative to each run's own peak, so a controller
  that lowers the peak gets a tighter band. Here every mode is scored in absolute terms:
  mean tool distance over the first second, and time until the tool stays within 0.5 mm.
- **Result** (taps at rest: two postures, two directions, seeds 32 and 33; mean distance
  / time to within 0.5 mm / peak):

| Variant | KX-6L | KX-6 | KX-6, budget actuators |
|---|---|---|---|
| Joint control only | 2.21 mm / 0.564 s / 13.1 mm | 1.77 mm / 0.506 s / 10.9 mm | 1.88 mm / 0.469 s / 11.5 mm |
| Joint-local | 1.09 mm / 0.266 s / 11.6 mm | 0.91 mm / 0.219 s / 9.4 mm | 1.19 mm / 0.976 s / 10.0 mm |
| Network | 1.14 mm / 0.216 s / 11.3 mm | 0.99 mm / 0.225 s / 9.4 mm | 1.26 mm / 0.582 s / 8.4 mm |
| Ideal | 0.39 mm / 0.077 s / 7.2 mm | 0.27 mm / 0.079 s / 5.2 mm | 0.48 mm / 1.525 s / 5.6 mm |
| Ideal, no tank | 0.35 mm / 0.075 s / 6.7 mm | 0.26 mm / 0.074 s / 5.2 mm | — |
| Ideal, one bus cycle late | 0.39 mm / 0.085 s / 7.2 mm | 0.28 mm / 0.079 s / 5.2 mm | — |

- **How much headroom.** After a tap, an ideal damper holds the tool 2.9× (KX-6L) to
  3.7× (KX-6) closer than network damping and is back within 0.5 mm 2.8× sooner, with
  the same torque and energy budget. That is the ceiling for any damper, learned or not.
- **Where it comes from.** Not from speed and not from giving up passivity: a bus
  cycle of delay and removing the tank change the ideal result by a few percent. It
  comes from acting on the whole arm's state at once, every joint's torque coordinated
  with every other's. Today each joint damps its own link from two gyros with one gain.
  Most of the state the ideal uses is already on the bus every cycle (every joint's
  motor and output angles and speeds, gear twist, gyro); what no joint measures is the
  bending angle of the links, only its rate.
- **Budget actuators.** The linear ideal controller, designed on the datasheet plant,
  lowers the mean distance but takes 1.5 s to stay within 0.5 mm, longer than joint
  control alone. Why was not investigated; gear play and stiction are the nonlinear
  parts a linear design ignores.
- **Tasks are not bounded here.** The ideal controller regulates to a fixed state, so it
  applies at rest. During tasks under a policy computer most of the vibration is the
  plan's own 15 Hz roughness (NX-25 band powers), which no damper should remove; a
  damper's share of task vibration is the arm's ring, which network damping already cuts
  by 40 %.
- **Recommendation.** There is real headroom (about 3×), but reinforcement learning is
  not the first way to get it. The gap is coordination on a known, nearly linear plant:
  a model-based coordinated damper (each joint computing its row of a gain like the ideal
  one from the states already on the bus, with an estimate of link bending) should reach
  much of it, and its stability can be argued like the current law's. Learning earns its
  place where the plant is not linear: gear play and stiction on budget actuators, where
  the linear ideal does worst. There, a policy trained against the joints' own learned models
  (the PHNN as the simulator, the energy tank kept as the guard) is the experiment worth
  doing next. Neither is built here.
