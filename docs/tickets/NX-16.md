## Log

- 2026-10-21: First design (high-passed IMU jolt and gear torque against a learned noise
  floor) failed on data. During normal tasks the housings see high-frequency jolts as
  large as, or larger than, a collision with a soft (person-like, 20 kN/m) obstacle: up to
  37 m/s² in a smooth figure of eight against 13 m/s² on contact. The largest values came
  from a policy-step bug, fixed separately. Feedback effort with inertia feed-forward did
  not separate either (the point-mass inertia is too rough).
- **What works: external torque from the dynamic chain.** Each node knows the specific
  force at both ends of its link (its own IMU and its outer neighbour's), so it can sum
  `m·f` from the tip inward over the bus, as the gravity chain sums `m·g`. That is the
  torque the measured motion needs. The gear twist says what the joint actually
  transmits. The difference is external torque: contact, measured by the body rather
  than predicted by a model. On the side collision, the base joint sees 93 N·m, against
  at most 27 N·m in any normal task (joint-level interpolation).
- **Commissioned thresholds** (`experiments/src/calibrate-impact.ts`): twice each joint's
  envelope over 4 tasks × 3 seeds with joint-level interpolation, at least 30 % of rated
  torque. KX-6: 53, 56, 85, 86, 3, 29 N·m. Central interpolation produced about twice the
  envelope (jerkier motion, larger model error) but still stayed below these thresholds.
  So joint-level interpolation also makes collision detection more sensitive.
- Results (KX-6 at home pose, 1 s side reach into the block, seed 61): contact at cycle
  22670, detection by joint 1 at 22779 (27 ms; the external torque had to build up
  against the soft block), every other joint reacting 1 cycle later (H3a met). H4: no
  false alarm in three tasks (seed 62, not used for commissioning).
- The host now sends its cyclic heartbeat frame and records faults and impacts; nodes
  hold position (no brakes) when the heartbeat stops for 5 ms.
- Landed together with NX-17 in one commit, because detector and supervisor share the
  node's safety path.
- Open: detection latency against soft obstacles (27 ms here) is set by the thresholds,
  which are set by model error. A better per-link inertia model (rotational inertia, the
  payload's centre of mass) would lower both.
