# NX-30: Estimator-based coordinated damping

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
NX-29 found that a damping gain seeing two joints either side matches the ideal
controller on the true state, but run as a static gain on the joints' bus data it
oscillates: its model treated the measurements as clean, delayed states. Asked for by
the owner: estimate the state with filters that model what is between the plant and the
gain, and design on that model.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] The linear model checked against the simulation before it is designed on: tap
  responses with damping off compared, and every known gap closed or named
- [x] A model with what NX-29 left out: bus latency on other joints' data, the cycle of
  actuation delay of the joints' torque, gyro bias; measurements only what is raw in the
  joints' frames (encoder angles, gyros)
- [x] A Kalman filter on that model and a gain designed on it; evaluated in simulation
  on bus data only: taps at the pose designed for and at another, both arms
- [x] Each joint's filter restricted to the joints up to two places away, if the full
  one holds up; in the node if that holds up, inside the energy tank and torque cap
- [x] Measured against network damping wherever it is built: taps, tasks, bus faults

## Tests

- `sim/src/analysis/estimator.test.ts`: the model's tap response within 10 % of the
  simulation's (light arm, home); on bus data, each joint filtering the frames of the
  joints up to two places away, a tap held to under 70 % of network damping's mean
  distance, and no oscillation left after it; in the joints (`DampingMode.coordinated`),
  a tap under 70 % of network damping's mean distance and a figure of eight tracked
  within 10 µm of network damping
- `crates/nexus-node/src/coordinated.rs`: a block is refused unless it fits; a step of
  one joint's angle gives the designed command
- `web/src/rig.test.ts`: the demo, from the committed design, holds a tap closer than
  network damping

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **The model first.** The held arm's linear model (NX-27 to NX-29) against
  the simulation, damping off, a vertical tap on the light arm: the model's tool came to
  rest far too soon (mean distance over a second 1.256 mm against 1.746; 0.36 against
  0.82 mm after half a second). Not the joints' cycle of actuation delay (1.268 mm with
  it), barely the IMU gravity feed-forward (1.651–1.685 mm in the simulation with it
  off or from encoders). The cause: the hinges' dry friction. Linearised at rest,
  MuJoCo's friction constraint is sticking and looks like an enormous damper; once a tap
  moves the arm, the joints slip. Taken out of the linearisation (`Plant.withoutFriction`)
  the model went unstable (the rotor–gear modes need some friction), so it is put back as
  equivalent viscous damping on the motor speeds, 10 N·m·s/rad per N·m of Coulomb
  friction, fitted once on the vertical tap: 1.712 against 1.746 mm; sideways, not
  fitted, 0.971 against 1.013 mm (error 0.13 mm RMS, from 0.73). Fitting the whole
  dynamics by least squares instead failed (a 30 % residual and an unstable model: the
  stiff gears). The models NX-27 to NX-29 designed on were overdamped, which is why
  aggressive gains on them oscillated.
- **The estimator-based damper** (`sim/src/analysis/estimator.ts`). On that model, with
  the joints' position loops and the damper each acting a cycle late, a steady-state
  Kalman predictor on what is raw in the frames (motor angle, twist, gyro; two cycles
  late), run at the measurements' time and carried forward with the commands since, and
  an LQR gain on the same model. Findings on the way:
  - The filter alone tracks the tool to 0.044 mm RMS during a 3 mm tap.
  - The ideal controller's weighting oscillates; 30 times its torque weight works.
  - At a posture between home and reach the gain drove the rotor–gear modes at
    200–300 Hz (J2 at 24 N·m RMS). A second-order 40 Hz low-pass on the command, in the
    model, fixed every posture.
  - Each joint's own filter on the frames of the joints up to two places away costs
    almost nothing against one filter on every frame. But the joints must know each
    other's commands at the measurements' time: a joint guessing them from its own
    estimate oscillated (their estimates drift apart). So each joint will publish its
    command in its state frame; only the last cycle is predicted.

  It holds up: 1.3–2× closer than network damping on the light arm, 2.4–3× on the stiff
  one, at least as fast to settle, on a lossy bus too, at postures it was not designed
  at. (Lossy bus: 20 % of frames lost, 4 cycles of jitter.) This runs outside the
  joints, on their frames as the host receives them; the node is next.

- **The model, again.** Damping the whole motor speed for friction (above) matched the
  tap at home but made the model unstable at the mid posture. Friction now damps only the
  fast part of the motor speed (`100·coulomb` on `v − LP(v)`, 20 Hz corner): the
  rotor–gear modes stay damped and the slow modes, where the joints slip during a tap,
  do not. Light arm, vertical tap, mean distance over a second, model against
  simulation: 1.860 against 1.746 mm at home, 4.669 against 4.327 at reach, 6.147 against
  6.230 between them. The results above were re-measured on it.
- **In the node** (`crates/nexus-node/src/coordinated.rs`, `DampingMode.coordinated`).
  The host loads each joint's block over the bus: which frames' channels it reads, the
  model, its filter (the joints up to two places away) and its row of the gain, about
  10 k numbers. Each joint publishes its state frames to every joint, with its
  coordinated command in the network-torque field; it keeps the latest frame of each
  joint within its reach and that joint's previous command. While the joint holds still,
  it averages where the arm rests and each gyro's bias (800 cycles), then runs its filter
  at the measurements' time, carries the estimate forward with the commands since, and
  applies its row of the gain, limited to its share of the torque cap before the 40 Hz
  low-pass (a limit after it wound up the filter's idea of the command), through the
  energy tank. It uses network damping while it moves, for 1000 cycles after, and when
  any frame it needs is more than 4 cycles old. Tried and dropped:
  - Feeding the filter frames up to 16 cycles old on the lossy bus: better at two
    postures, oscillating at the third (commands and measurements no longer belong to
    the same cycle).
  - Falling back after 80 cycles at the torque cap: worse everywhere.
  - A 0.5 Hz high-pass on the command, against a steady offset after a tap in the demo
    (below): worse.
- **The demo.** On the stiff arm the coordinated mode left the tool 0.6 mm off after a
  tap, held there. Not the damper: the committed design was made with the arm's spec as
  it is, 3 kg at the tool, while the demo starts with no payload, and the demo's check
  for a matching design compared the slider against 0 instead of against the design.
  The design now records its payload, the demo's designs are made for the arm the demo
  starts with, and any other payload falls back to network damping. Demo tap, first
  second: KX-6 0.274 mm against network damping's 0.646; KX-6L 0.457 against 0.851; both
  back within 0.02 mm.
