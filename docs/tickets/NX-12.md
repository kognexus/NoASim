# NX-12: Report to source, correction, fault (B1b)

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Fingerprint ID, DisturbanceReport, adaptive ripple compensation at the source, deadline then FaultReport to host
- [x] H5 and H7 measured

## Tests

- `crates/nexus-node/src/source.rs`: the demodulator locks onto a tone at its phase; a
  joint whose own residual carries a ripple at one of its orders claims it
- `crates/nexus-proto/tests/golden.rs` and `sim/src/bus/protocol.test.ts`: the new
  `Disturbance` frame and the fingerprint in `Announce`, byte for byte in both codecs
- `sim/src/harness/source.test.ts`: with every joint moving, only the source claims a
  gear-mesh ripple; a compensable ripple is cancelled (helper effort under 10 %) before the
  deadline without a fault; with correction off, or with a ripple beyond the compensation
  budget, the source faults within the deadline
- `experiments/src/report-to-source.ts`: H5 and the 100-trial H7 run (numbers below)

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **Wire.** `Announce` now carries the joint's fingerprint: the four orders
  (cycles per output turn) at which it can excite the arm. These are once per motor turn,
  the gear mesh (taken as four per motor turn), the sixth electrical harmonic, and once
  per output turn. A new `Disturbance` frame (source, order, amplitude) is broadcast.
  Fault code `Source` (4) goes to the host.
- **First design, measured and dropped: neighbours name the source from the vibration they
  feel.** Every node demodulated its high-passed gear torque at each candidate's orders,
  locked to that candidate's published motor angle, and named the most coherent
  candidate. On the KX-6 this got 3/20 right (13 wrong). Every KX-6 joint has the same
  fingerprint (gear ratio 9), so only the angle tells candidates apart. At working
  speeds the gear-mesh order lands at 5–10 Hz, on the arm's own lightly damped modes,
  which ring coherently at the order frequency of any joint moving at a similar speed.
  Narrower demodulators, longer settling, coherence and margin tests helped little.
  Without any injected ripple, joint 1 still "felt" joint 2 at 11 N·m.
- **What works: each joint tests itself, on its motor-side residual.** The residual is the
  torque the motor really produced (`I·α − gear torque + friction`) minus the command
  that acted. Vibration of the arm appears on both sides of that balance and cancels, so
  the residual holds only what arises inside the actuator. Three details mattered:
  - the command is aligned with the acceleration it caused (one cycle of actuation delay);
  - α is taken from the raw encoder's second difference rather than the 150 Hz observer,
    whose lag leaked the large balancing torque into the residual;
  - every term passes the same 150 Hz band-limit.

  With the report threshold at 3 % of rated torque (2 % let the shoulder's residual leak
  through), self-tests named the source 20/20 in joints-alone mode. They also found a real
  sixth-harmonic commutation ripple on joint 1 (order 864, from the Hall commutation).
- **Final design: felt plus claimed.** Neighbours still demodulate what they feel and
  broadcast *felt* reports. A joint *claims* a disturbance (a report naming itself) when
  its own residual shows the order. In the network modes it also needs a felt report at
  that order from another joint in the last 2 s: the vibration must reach the arm, not just
  the joint's own balance. The felt report need not name the claimant, since the residual
  is what tells who the source is. Requiring it to name the claimant lost 5/20 true
  claims, because felt naming is unreliable.
- **Correction** (`NetworkWithSource` mode): the claimant demodulates its residual at the
  claimed order and feeds the opposite forward. Two corrections were needed:
  - the residual's known delays (one cycle, plus the band-limit filter's
    `2·atan(ω/ωc)/ω`) and its gain loss are compensated, and the anti-ripple is aimed
    1.5 cycles ahead. Without that, a 41 Hz ripple was only 35 % cancelled;
  - the feed-forward is capped at 20 % of rated torque, as drives cap anti-cogging. A
    larger ripple (a damaged gear) cannot be cancelled.
- **Deadline and fault.** If another joint still feels the claimed order more than 2 s
  after the first claim, the claimant reports `Fault(Source)` to the host. A joint alone
  never faults, since it can't tell whether the arm still shakes. A worn bearing (pulses
  at random positions) was tried as the non-compensable case and dropped. Its pulses are
  incoherent, so neither the residual nor the neighbours see a locked component. Anything
  coherent enough to be felt can also be cancelled, so the honest non-compensable case is
  a ripple beyond the compensation budget.
- **H5** (`experiments/src/report-to-source.ts`; KX-6, joint 3 sweeping at 0.3 rad/s,
  ripple at the sixth electrical harmonic, order 864, ≈ 41 Hz):

| Case | First claim | Fault at host | Helper effort before | Under 10 % |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 % rated, corrected | 1.85 s | none | 0.445 N·m | 0.40 s after the claim |
| 10 % rated, correction off | 1.85 s | 3.90 s (2.05 s after the claim) | 0.445 N·m | — |
| 50 % rated (beyond budget), corrected | 2.05 s | 4.10 s (2.05 s after the claim) | 0.887 N·m | — |

  The source's own gear torque at the order drops from ≈ 9 N·m to ≈ 1 N·m within 0.25 s of
  the claim. **H5 met**: the compensable case is fixed well before the 2 s deadline, and the
  non-compensable one reaches the host as a fault one report period after it.
- **H7** (100 blinded trials, KX-6): random source joint, gear-mesh ripple of 5–15 % of
  rated torque, every joint stroking at its own speed (1.0–1.6 rad/s, smooth reversals
  every 1.2 s). A trial is right if the source, and only the source, claims it.

| Mode | Right | Wrong | Missed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network | 100/100 | 0 | 0 |
| Joints alone (local) | 100/100 | 0 | 0 |

  **H7's ≥ 97/100 is met.** Its second half, "fewer errors than joints alone", is **not met**:
  both modes use the same self-test and make no errors, so the network can't do better
  here. What the network adds over a joint alone is the corroboration (that the
  disturbance reaches the arm), the correction, and the fault verdict, which a joint alone
  can't make. At the looser 2 % threshold, corroboration did cut false claims
  (2 against 5 in 10 trials), so it matters once self-tests become marginal, for example on
  hidden actuators. NX-15 reruns H7 there.

- Not done here: the plan's "residual for attribution" with a learned model is NX-15.
  The residual already uses the nominal inertia and friction model; the PHNN should
  sharpen it on actuators that differ from the datasheet.
