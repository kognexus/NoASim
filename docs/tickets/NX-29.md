# NX-29: Coordinated damping from joints two places away

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
NX-27 found about 3× headroom over network damping after a tap, all of it from
coordinating the joints; NX-28 found that an ideal gain cut to two joints either side
keeps most of it. Asked for by the owner: build that damper.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] A damping gain designed for the structure: joint i uses only the states of the
  joints up to two places away, optimised on the linearised arm (not an LQR gain cut)
- [x] Evaluated first with the true state, then with only what the joints have: their
  own sensors, the others' states over the bus a cycle or more late, and link bending
  estimated from the gyros
- [x] If it holds up, in the node (it does not hold up on bus data yet: not built; see
  the log): each joint listens to the joints up to two places
  away, runs its row of the gain, inside the energy tank and torque cap, and falls back
  to network damping when data is missing
- [ ] Measured against network damping: taps (both arms, both postures, unseen seeds)
  done; task tracking and vibration under both computers, bus latency, jitter and loss
  (H6) not measured, since the damper is not built
- [x] Gains for a posture other than the one designed at: measured, not assumed

## Tests

- `sim/src/util/matrix.test.ts` (from NX-28) and `sim/src/analysis/structured.test.ts`:
  the discrete Lyapunov solver, and on the light arm a gain for two joints either side
  within 5 % of the full gain's cost, with every entry outside the structure zero

## Log

- 2026-10-21: **Designing for the structure** (`sim/src/analysis/structured.ts`). The
  LQR cost of NX-27, averaged over taps in two directions, minimised over gains whose
  row i is zero outside the joints within `reach` of i. Plain gradient steps were badly
  conditioned (after 150 steps the cost was twice that of simply cutting the full gain),
  so it starts from the cut gain and takes Gauss–Newton (policy-iteration) steps masked
  to the structure, with a line search that keeps every gain stabilising. It can only
  improve on the cut gain; a design takes under a second.
- **On the true state the structure costs almost nothing** (taps; gains designed at
  home; mean distance over the first second, time to stay within 0.5 mm):

| | KX-6L home | KX-6L reach | KX-6 home | KX-6 reach |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Network damping (today) | 0.721 mm, 0.17 s | 1.549 mm, 0.26 s | 0.667 mm, 0.19 s | 1.314 mm, 0.26 s |
| Designed for ±1 joint | 0.239 mm, 0.07 s | 0.626 mm, 0.14 s | 0.178 mm, 0.06 s | 0.417 mm, 0.10 s |
| Designed for ±2 joints | 0.217 mm, 0.06 s | 0.586 mm, 0.14 s | 0.143 mm, 0.05 s | 0.370 mm, 0.10 s |
| ±2, 3 cycles late (designed for it) | 0.310 mm, 0.06 s | 1.126 mm, 1.56 s | 0.327 mm, 0.06 s | 0.534 mm, 0.13 s |
| ±2, from the bus alone | 0.476 mm, 0.17 s | 1.793 mm, 1.61 s | 0.669 mm, not within 3 s | 0.926 mm, not within 3 s |

  Even neighbours only, designed for it, comes within 10–25 % of the ideal; this
  corrects NX-28, whose slow tail for neighbours only came from cutting the full gain,
  not from the structure. Gains designed at home work at the other pose too, on the true
  state.
- **From what the joints have** (`sim/src/analysis/coordinated.ts`). No joint measures
  link bending, but a housing's integrated gyro is its orientation, linear near the pose
  in every angle up the chain; so per joint: motor angle, twist, motor speed, twist rate,
  gyro, integrated gyro, from the state frames as they arrive. The state gain is fitted
  onto these measurements and refined through `K = F·C`. What it took to get this far:
  - The twist rate on the bus (output minus motor speed, both from observers) is off by
    100–200 %; the motor speed lags 7–8 cycles, and a gain using it settled into a
    40–90 Hz oscillation. Both left out; the gyros carry the motion with little lag.
  - A gyro bias taken from one sample at switch-on, integrated, held the arm off by
    itself; averaged over 0.2 s first.
  - The fitted gain was unstable once the model had delay; it is scaled down until
    stable, then refined. The sensor noise (measured per channel) is in the cost.
  - Results above: better than network damping at the light arm's home pose (−34 % mean,
    same settling), worse or oscillating elsewhere. Designing one gain for both poses at
    once made it oscillate at both and at a third.
- **Why, and the decision.** On the true state with delay the same designs settle; on
  bus data they do not. The model treats the measurements as clean, delayed states:
  the joints' observers, the gyros' integration with its leak and bias, and the position
  loops' own cycle of actuation delay are not in it, and aggressive gains find the
  difference. The criterion was "if it holds up, in the node"; it does not yet, so it is
  not built. The next step is an estimator-based design: each joint's neighbourhood
  estimated by a filter that models those dynamics, with the gain designed on that
  model.
