# NX-31: Coordinated models, learned from the neighbourhood

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
Asked for by the owner: retrain each joint's learned model (NX-14) so that it also learns
from its nearest neighbours' motion, and find out whether the network of actuators then
does better on (1) settling time, (2) tracking error and (3) full-body stabilisation.

Each joint's learned model so far (`docs/phnn.md`) is a model of its actuator alone,
commissioned while the other joints hold. It explains the motor side of the gear; what
the gear has to carry, the arm beyond it, it takes as measured. The joints' control has
no inertial feed-forward at all: proportional–derivative plus friction and gravity. How
hard a joint must push to follow its reference depends on how its neighbours accelerate
(the arm's mass matrix couples them), and no joint knows that today.

## Assumptions (the owner was away; recorded so they can be overruled)

- "Data variations from nearest neighbours": during commissioning the neighbours move
  too, each on its own excitation, and what they publish is an input of the joint's
  model.
- "The motion of the current actuator": the torque the joint's gear must transmit for the
  motion of its neighbourhood, `τ_i = Σ_j M_ij(q) q̈_j + (velocity terms)` over the joints
  up to two places away. The same model, inverted, gives the joint's acceleration for a
  torque; the forward form is the one a joint can use for feed-forward.
- The structure stays physical, as the project's modelling choice asks: the
  neighbourhood's inertia is positive definite by construction (`M = L·Lᵀ`), and the
  joint's torque is its row of the Euler–Lagrange equations. Gravity stays with the
  gravity chain (B10), which also handles payload. The actuator models stay as they are.
- "Full-body stabilisation": the whole arm's response to disturbances while holding (a
  push and a sideways shake at the tool) and the vibration all joints feel during tasks.
- Arms: KX-6L and KX-6 with datasheet actuators (the demo's default), designed and
  measured at several postures.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] Headroom first: an oracle that adds the inertial torque from the simulator's mass
  matrix and the joints' references as the bus carries them: the joint's own row
  diagonal only, its neighbours up to one and two places away, the whole arm. For each
  of the three measures, whether any of it helps; a measure with no headroom is reported
  and not built for
- [x] Neighbourhood commissioning: all joints excite at once on independent seeds, at
  several postures; each joint's log is what it published and what it received from the
  joints up to two places away
- [x] A coordinated model per joint, trained only from its own log; an own-only model
  (no neighbours) trained on the same data as the baseline; held-out error of both
- [x] In the node if it holds up: loaded over the bus, each joint publishing its
  reference in its state frame; the feed-forward of its row inside the torque limits;
  stale neighbour data falls back to the own-only term
- [x] Measured against today's joints, the own-only model and the oracle: settling after
  a tap, tracking under the VLA and the classic computer, and full-body stabilisation

## Tests

  Inertial feed-forward is worth 10–15 % on the light arm and up to 40 % on the stiff
  one; almost all of it is each joint's own inertia. The neighbours add up to 12 % more
  (stiff arm, classic computer) and nothing, or a little worse, under the VLA computer.
  Joint following error drops 40 % with the neighbours, but on the light arm most of the
  tool's error is not following error.
  - **Where the light arm's error is:** 1.5 mm of its 1.8–2.5 mm is a steady offset,
    present at rest too: the links sag under gravity (1.48 mm of the 1.50 mm hold offset
    goes when the bends are taken out of the kinematics) while every joint holds its
    angle exactly. If each joint shifted by half its own link's bend and half its inner
    neighbour's (exact for a bend at mid-length, as the simulation models links), the hold
    offset would fall to 0.26 mm. On the stiff arm sag is 0.18 of 0.33 mm, and the same
    shifts do nothing useful. No joint sees its own link's bend: the next joint's IMU does.
  - **Settling:** feed-forward does nothing at rest (the references are still).
    Cancelling the coupling instead, each joint adding `Σ_{j≠i} M_ij · q̈_j` from the
    measured accelerations two cycles late, is unstable even with the true values
    (an algebraic acceleration loop): the arm diverged within seconds. Not built.
  - **Absolute tilt is not measurable:** an accelerometer bias of 0.02 g is a tilt
    error of about 20 mrad, ten times the bends. The bend *rate* is: the next joint's gyro
    minus the joint's own and its speed, on its axis (the network-damping signal); gyro
    bias drops out once the rate is band-passed.
  - **So the coordinated model is two things per joint:** its row of the neighbourhood's
    inertia (feed-forward, tracking) and its link's compliance, learned from the bend
    rate against the rate of its gear torque (sag, tracking and full-body accuracy). At
    run time both are feed-forward from torques and references the joints already know.
- **Neighbourhood commissioning** (`experiments/src/commission-neighbourhood.ts`): every
  joint runs its own excitation at once, 20 s for training and 10 s held out, at ten
  postures. Three things had to change before the learned rows matched the simulator's
  mass matrix:
  - The joints' excitations shared one band with phases at most π/2 apart, so the
    neighbours moved nearly in phase and a joint's own-only model took their inertia for
    its own (twice the true diagonal). Each joint's frequencies are now interleaved with
    the others' (`Commission::frequency_scale`).
  - Fitted freely, a row picked up dependence on joints whose angle cannot enter it. It
    now follows the serial-arm structure: `m_ij` depends only on the joints beyond the
    inner of `i` and `j`.
  - Three postures were too few: the excitation moves each joint only about ±0.1 rad (its
    speed budget), and between postures, or with the wrist turned, the rows went wrong
    (J3's diagonal 2.5 against 1.4 along pick and place). Ten postures, two of them with
    the base and wrist turned, fixed it.
  On the stiff arm the learned rows now match the mass matrix to a few percent at and
  between the commissioned postures. Each joint's link compliance from the gyros is
  within 3 % of the true one on the light arm's two flexible links; the rigid links fit
  nothing (under 0.1 of the bend's variance), and are taken as rigid.
- **In the node** (`crates/nexus-node/src/coordinated_model.rs`, `Settings::
  coordinated_model`). Two findings on the way:
  - The interpolators' reference accelerations spike (a setpoint arriving one cycle after
    the last, quintic segments ending at zero acceleration: up to 100 rad/s² against
    about 1 rad/s² RMS). Fed forward from five joints they cost more than the coupling
    gained. Each joint now publishes the smoothed derivative of its reference velocity
    (corner a sixth of its goal rate, 2–10 Hz) and everyone feeds that forward.
  - With sag compensation on, the classic controller found the joints 1–2 mrad off the
    rigid inverse kinematics and inserted a 0.3 s lead-in, so its task ran behind the
    clock it was scored against (62 mm "error"). Joints now publish their commanded
    reference (before their own shift) and tasks start from it.

- **Answers to the owner's three questions:**
  1. *Settling:* the coordinated model does not change it; decoupling the joints by their
     measured accelerations is unstable even with the true mass matrix. What settles
     faster through the network is NX-30's coordinated damping (1.8–2.8× closer after a
     tap here), and the two combine without interfering.
  2. *Tracking:* yes. On the light arm the coordinated model cuts tool error 24–64 %
     (classic computer, figure of eight: 1.68 → 0.61 mm), most of it from sag that only
     the next joint's gyro reveals; the joint alone gets 9–11 %. On the stiff arm each
     joint's own inertia does most of it (14–41 %), the neighbours add up to 16 % more.
  3. *Full-body stabilisation:* static accuracy, yes: the light arm's tool sits 3–7×
     closer to where it should be while holding, at every posture measured. Disturbance
     rejection comes from coordinated damping, not the model.
- **Found on the way, fixed:** with NX-30's coordinated damping, classic pick and place
  tracked worse (1.07 against 0.87 mm on the light arm, 1.01 against 0.71 on the stiff
  one). Not the pauses: a joint judged "holding" by its own reference alone, so one that
  stands still during a move (the base while the arm lifts) engaged its whole-arm damper
  and calibrated against a moving arm, for 2.9 s of an 8 s task. The damper now engages
  only while every joint's published reference velocity is zero; pick and place then
  tracks as without it (0.870 and 0.711 mm), and taps, holds, shake and push are
  unchanged (Estimator and Neighbourhood re-measured).
- **Found on the way, not fixed:** Task vibration rises about 10 % with the own-only model (its
  inertia is a little high: it still takes in part of its neighbours'), not with the
  coordinated one. Starting tasks from the joints' references moved the hidden-actuator
  rows of the proof by 1–2 %, except the budget unit with datasheet models, whose tap
  after its tasks settles in 0.257 s instead of 0.505 s.
- **In the demo** (asked for by the owner after the ticket closed). The demo starts with no
  payload, so its arms get their own models: `commission-neighbourhood.ts --payload 0`,
  trained into `models/coordinated-models/<arm>-payload0-reach2/`. Without a payload the
  links carry too little torque for their bend to stand out of the gyro noise (fit 0.09
  and 0.01, where rigid links score up to 0.02), so the compliance is learned from the
  commissioning with the 3 kg payload (`--compliance-from`): it is a property of the link,
  not of what the arm carries. "Coordinated models" in the model menu runs them on top of
  the datasheet model, for the arm as it starts (datasheet actuators, no payload, links as
  specified); otherwise that entry is the datasheet model alone. In the demo's light arm:
  hold 0.54 → 0.18 mm; figure of eight 0.60 → 0.32 mm (classic computer) and 1.50 → 1.34 mm
  (VLA); stiff arm 0.40 → 0.28 mm (classic), its hold unchanged at 0.13 mm.
