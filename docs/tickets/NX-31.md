# NX-31: Coordinated models, learned from the neighbourhood

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
Asked for by the owner: retrain each joint's learned model (NX-14) so that it also learns
from its nearest neighbours' motion, and find out whether the network of actuators then
does better on (1) settling time, (2) tracking error and (3) full-body stabilisation.

Each joint's learned model so far (`docs/phnn.md`) is a model of its actuator alone,
commissioned while the other joints hold. It explains the motor side of the gear; what
the gear has to carry, the arm beyond it, it takes as measured. The joints' control has
no inertial feed-forward at all: proportional–derivative plus friction and gravity. How
hard a joint must push to follow its reference depends on how its neighbours accelerate
(the arm's mass matrix couples them), and no joint knows that today.

## Assumptions (the owner was away; recorded so they can be overruled)

- "Data variations from nearest neighbours": during commissioning the neighbours move
  too, each on its own excitation, and what they publish is an input of the joint's
  model.
- "The motion of the current actuator": the torque the joint's gear must transmit for the
  motion of its neighbourhood, `τ_i = Σ_j M_ij(q) q̈_j + (velocity terms)` over the joints
  up to two places away. The same model, inverted, gives the joint's acceleration for a
  torque; the forward form is the one a joint can use for feed-forward.
- The structure stays physical, as the project's modelling choice asks: the
  neighbourhood's inertia is positive definite by construction (`M = L·Lᵀ`), and the
  joint's torque is its row of the Euler–Lagrange equations. Gravity stays with the
  gravity chain (B10), which also handles payload. The actuator models stay as they are.
- "Full-body stabilisation": the whole arm's response to disturbances while holding (a
  push and a sideways shake at the tool) and the vibration all joints feel during tasks.
- Arms: KX-6L and KX-6 with datasheet actuators (the demo's default), designed and
  measured at several postures.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Headroom first: an oracle that adds the inertial torque from the simulator's mass
  matrix and the joints' references as the bus carries them: the joint's own row
  diagonal only, its neighbours up to one and two places away, the whole arm. For each
  of the three measures, whether any of it helps; a measure with no headroom is reported
  and not built for
- [ ] Neighbourhood commissioning: all joints excite at once on independent seeds, at
  several postures; each joint's log is what it published and what it received from the
  joints up to two places away
- [ ] A coordinated model per joint, trained only from its own log; an own-only model
  (no neighbours) trained on the same data as the baseline; held-out error of both
- [ ] In the node if it holds up: loaded over the bus, each joint publishing its
  reference in its state frame; the feed-forward of its row inside the torque limits;
  stale neighbour data falls back to the own-only term
- [ ] Measured against today's joints, the own-only model and the oracle: settling after
  a tap, tracking under the VLA and the classic computer, and full-body stabilisation
