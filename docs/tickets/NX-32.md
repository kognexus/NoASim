# NX-32: A colleague's frame-aligned damping law, tested without a tank

See [PLAN.md](../PLAN.md) for the behaviours (B*) and hypotheses (H*) referenced here.
A colleague's Lyapunov derivation (`lyapunov_qdd.pdf`, shared by the owner) argues that
network damping on frame-aligned IMU rates drains energy unconditionally:

```text
Δω_ij = ω_i − R_ij(q)·ω_j          (j's gyro resolved in i's frame by the encoders)
τ_i   = −d_i·θ̇_m,i − Σ_{j∈N(i)} D_ij · HPF(Δω_ij)
V̇     = −q̇ᵀ D_local q̇ − Δωᵀ L Δω ≤ 0
```

ADR-0004 holds that a joint's torque on a rate measured across its link is not
collocated, so its power has no fixed sign, and that the energy tank is what bounds it.
The owner asked to settle this by running the law as written.

## Acceptance criteria

- [x] The law as written, outside the joints, on the simulator's gyros resolved in each
  joint's frame by its encoder angles, high-passed, both neighbours, no energy tank
- [x] Swept over gain (a quarter to eight times the network gain) and bus delay (0, 2,
  4 cycles), both arms: the energy each joint's term puts in, tap settling, stability
- [x] The no-delay case on the linearised arm: whether the closed loop is stable
- [x] Recorded in ADR-0004 whichever way it falls

## Log

- 2026-10-21: Ticket written.
- **Frame alignment checked first** (`sim/src/analysis/frameAligned.ts`): with only the
  base turning, every resolved relative rate is zero; with one joint turning at 1 rad/s,
  its outer edge reads exactly its own speed plus its link's bend rate, and its inner
  edge the inner joint's. So the law sees what the note says it sees.
- **As written, the outer term is `+D·(q̇_i + ḃ_i)`:** it pushes the joint along its own
  speed. The note's signs are inconsistent (its `τ_damping` carries a minus and enters the
  equations of motion with another), so a flipped variant, with every term a damper on
  its relative rate, was run too, and the outer edge alone (the shape of our network
  damping, without its tank, relay or posture scaling).
- **Result** (`experiments/src/frame-aligned.ts`; vertical tap at home; mean distance
  over a second; energy each term put in or took out; with the joints' IMU low-pass):

| | KX-6L, 0.5× | KX-6L, 1× | KX-6L, 8× | KX-6, 0.5× | KX-6, 1× | KX-6, 8× |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Joint control only | 1.738 mm | | | 1.404 mm | | |
| Network damping, with its tank | | 0.866 mm | | | 0.750 mm | |
| As written | 1.693 mm, +121 mJ | unstable | unstable | 1.486 mm, +89 mJ | unstable | unstable |
| Flipped | 1.934 mm, +22 mJ | 2.042 mm, +69 mJ | unstable | 1.518 mm, +10 mJ | 1.640 mm, +41 mJ | unstable |
| Flipped, outer edge only | 1.219 mm, −98 mJ | 0.947 mm, −150 mJ | 1.938 mm, +916 mJ | 1.012 mm, −69 mJ | 0.813 mm, −106 mJ | 0.786 mm, −138 mJ |

  Delay (2 and 4 cycles) changes these little, except at the edge: at 8× the outer edge
  goes unstable on the light arm with 4 cycles, and puts 43 mJ in on the stiff one.
  Without the IMU low-pass (the note has none) the rotor–gear modes join in: the outer
  edge alone is unstable on the light arm from 1×, and the law as written from 0.5×.
  On the linearised arm with no delay and no filters, the closed loop as written is
  unstable from 1× (growth 39 /s on the light arm, 29 /s on the stiff one) and the outer
  edge alone from 2× (35 /s and, at 4×, 25 /s); the linear model calls a few cases stable
  that diverge in the simulation (its gear modes are its least accurate part, NX-30).
- **Conclusion:** the derivation's claim `V̇ ≤ 0` unconditionally does not hold for the law
  as a joint can apply it. The outer-edge damper is dissipative at moderate gains in
  practice, which is what our network damping uses, and the energy tank is what makes
  it safe beyond them. ADR-0004 updated.
